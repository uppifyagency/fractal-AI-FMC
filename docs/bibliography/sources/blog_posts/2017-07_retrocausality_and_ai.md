# Retrocausality and AI

## Main Post Content

**Posted:** Thursday, 6 July 2017 by Sergio Hernandez

### Overview

The blog post explores the concept of retrocausality—the idea that physical events in the present can affect events in the past—and its potential applications to artificial intelligence design.

### Key Arguments

The author discusses how retrocausality, while counterintuitive, aligns with fundamental physics principles. According to the post, "if the time symmetry found in all known physic laws is to be accepted as a fundamental law... then causality must go on both directions too."

The author references a phys.org article about retrocausal quantum theory, noting that accepting this framework could resolve several quantum mechanics puzzles including action-at-distance, non-locality, and Bell's theorem.

### Connection to AI

The post describes the author's previous experimentation with a "Feynman fractal AI" model where signals traveled bidirectionally through time. The author acknowledged this approach was theoretically elegant but offered no practical improvement over standard fractal AI methods.

### Speculative Neural Network Design

The author proposes the possibility of neural networks where learning occurs continuously during operation, with signals at different time-shifts interacting. Rather than relying on gradient-based learning, this approach would involve penalizing past neural pathways when incorrect outputs occur.

### Conclusion

The author emphasizes this direction only makes sense "if and only if the universe has a T-symmetry," acknowledging the speculative nature of the proposal.
